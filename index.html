For marathon runners and the people who love them

Get your people to the right kilometre.

A marathon is a very long way to run on your own. Sideline lets your friends see the whole course, pick a spot, say what they are bringing, and follow you live, so the hard part is never empty.

Why this exists

Support goes a long way. Organising it goes nowhere.

If you have ever tried to get friends out along a marathon course, you know how it goes. A group chat, a screenshot of a map, a lot of "where are you". Three people end up at the same corner and the runner does the hardest ten kilometres with nobody there.

The support is real. There is just no way to see it, plan it or share it. Sideline is that missing piece.

Who is standing where, what are they bringing, and where is the runner right now?

What it does

Everything the squad needs on one screen.

Who stands where

Cheering spots on the real course, the official ones included, with the train line to get there. Friends pick one and their face shows up on the route.

Where the gaps are

The whole course folds into a snake with everyone's face on it. The empty stretches show up, especially the brutal bit after 30 km.

What they bring

Water, gels, a banana, a jacket for the finish, a big sign, noise. Big buttons, so nobody brings three bananas and no water.

The runner, live

From the runner's phone, or from the official race tracking where the race has it. Everyone gets an arrival time for their own corner.

"I just saw them!"

One tap when the runner goes past. The whole squad gets told, the route turns green, and everyone can pile in with cheers.

Chat and a photo wall

One chat for the squad and every photo in one place, with reactions and comments. Friends who can't be there follow along from home.

How it works

Three steps, no app store.

The runner makes a squad

Pick the race, set a goal time, and share one link with everyone who is coming.

Friends pick a spot

Open the link, tap a spot on the map, type a first name. No account, no password.

Race day runs itself

Live position, arrival times for every corner, "I just saw them" and photos, all in one place.

Where it is now

On the web today. Phones next.

Live on the webAndroid, sooniPhone, soon

Sideline works right now in any phone browser, and you can add it to your home screen so it opens like an app. The Berlin Marathon is the first race on it, with more in the queue.

Proper Android and iPhone apps are on the way. The phone apps matter most for runners, because they can keep sharing the runner's position with the screen locked in a belt.

Who made this

Hi, I'm Ermis.

I studied computer science and then spent years doing pretty much everything except writing code. These days I build things with AI doing most of the typing and me doing most of the deciding. I am a hobbyist, not really a coder, and I have made my peace with that.

Sideline started with a friend running Berlin and one question nobody could answer: who is going to be where? It has been a great excuse to use a part of my head that sat quiet for a long time. Most of the fun was deciding what it should feel like, not how it should work.

If you try it on a race, I would love to hear how it went. There is a feedback box inside the app, and it goes straight to me.
